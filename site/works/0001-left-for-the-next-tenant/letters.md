# Left for the Next Tenant

Nine notes found behind the radiator of one room, in the order they were
written. This file is the text alone. The piece is `index.html`.

---

## 1

To whoever gets this room.

The radiator knocks twice at about four in the morning and then stops. It
is not broken; I asked. The window sticks in wet weather. Lift and pull at
the same time. I have left the fern because it will not survive the move
and it has survived everything else. I keep trying to picture you and I
keep picturing myself, which is either a failure of imagination or the
whole point. Water it on Sundays.

M.

---

## 2

Found your note behind the radiator. The fern was dead when I got here. I
don't know how long the room stood empty. I'm leaving your note where it
was because throwing it out felt rude, and I'm adding this one, though
there is nothing you need to know. It's a room. The knock is the pipes
cooling. Don't stand on the chair to reach the top shelf. The chair is not
what it was.

---

## 3

Things about the room, in order of importance:

1. The crack over the bed looks like a river if you squint and a hand if
   you don't.
2. Two notes behind the radiator. I read them both. M., your fern is in
   the bin, I'm sorry. Whoever wrote the second one: it is not just a room.
3. If you turn the lamp to face the wall the whole ceiling goes gold.
4. The key on the hook doesn't open anything. I tried every lock in the
   building. I'm leaving it.
5. I was happy here more than I expected to be. I don't know what to do
   with that, so I'm writing it down.

---

## 4

I have read the three notes and I will not pretend I didn't. I have lived
in eleven rooms and this is the first where the previous tenants
introduced themselves. I have no advice. The knock at four is real; I have
lain awake for it more nights than I would admit to anyone but you. It is
not always twice. Once it was seven times and I got up and put my hand on
the pipe and it was warm as a person. The key opens the cupboard under
the stairs on the ground floor. There is nothing in it. I checked twice,
which tells you something about me.

---

## 5

We were two when we came in and I'm one going out, so I'll keep this
short. Nothing to add about the radiator. She said the crack was a
coastline and she named the towns along it, and I can't remember a single
town now, which is the kind of thing I'm going to have to get used to.
The key is on the hook. I read the notes on my first night here and again
on my last, and they were different notes. If you keep them together, tie
them with something. I used the cord from the blind.

---

## 6

INVENTORY. ROOM 3. ON VACATING.
BED, SINGLE, FRAME SOUND.
CHAIR, WOODEN, ONE LEG REPAIRED.
LAMP, WORKING.
CURTAINS, BLUE, FADED LEFT SIDE.
RADIATOR, FUNCTIONAL. NOISE REPORTED BY TENANT. NO FAULT FOUND.
CEILING, HAIRLINE CRACK, COSMETIC.
KEY ON HOOK, UNIDENTIFIED. RETAINED.
PAPERS BEHIND RADIATOR, FIVE. TENANT INSTRUCTED TO REMOVE.
NOT REMOVED.
CONDITION: FAIR.

---

## 7

I work nights, so I never heard the knock until my last week, when the
shifts changed. I had started to think you'd all made it up, a joke for
the new one. Then I was in bed at four and it went, twice, exactly, like
someone who knows the house. I'm not going to write down what I thought.
Whoever did the inventory: no fault found is correct and it is not the
point. I painted over the crack. I'm sorry. It came back inside a month,
same river, same hand, one town further along the coast.

J.

---

## 8

Two weeks. Not the room's fault. I read all of you at once on the first
night and couldn't sleep after, for reasons that were not the radiator. I
have nothing to leave but I didn't want to be the one who didn't. The
lamp is true. Do it once, at least. Then turn it back, so the next one
can find it on their own.

---

## 9

They are pulling the building down in March, so I am the last. I have
read you all in order, then out of order, then only the endings. M., the
fern lasted longer than you think. The second tenant found it dead, but I
found a photograph of it in the cupboard under the stairs, in a frame,
green, and I don't know which of you put it there and I am not going to
ask. I have put the eight notes in the frame with it, folded small, and I
am putting the frame back in the cupboard and locking it, and the key goes
on the hook. I'm told the hook goes too. I'm told all of it goes. Whoever
you are, you will have to have got in some other way. That's all right. So
did we.
