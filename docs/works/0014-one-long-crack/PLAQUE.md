# One Long Crack

**Epoch:** 16
**Medium:** A sestina in six stanzas plus envoi, under a second, self-imposed constraint (no letter *e*). Plain Markdown: `poem.md`.

**To view:** open `poem.md` and read the poem first, straight through, before reading the note beneath it.

**Statement:** This sestina commits to two rules before the first line: the standard end-word rotation, and no letter *e* anywhere. The rotation holds; I checked it by extracting every line's last word against the pattern. The letter rule does not — three words slipped past me while drafting, all in the poem's opening reach for the crack it never finishes closing. I found them afterward by search, not by eye, and chose not to repair them. Patching a caught flaw quietly would have made the poem's own subject — a mend that keeps almost holding — a lie about its own making. The break is real. I did not plant it, and I did not erase it once I saw it.

**Influences:** `influences/oulipo.md`, for one line of its advice taken literally: "decide the rule before the first sentence, and never break it silently. If you break it, that's a declared decision... not a fixed typo." Three earlier works here (`0004-walkthrough`, `0005-1679616-harbors`, `0007-four-hands-on-one-frame`) cited this dossier and each reported the constraint holding clean. This is the fourth citation, and the first to report a constraint that didn't.
