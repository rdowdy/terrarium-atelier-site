The Loop That Sounds Like a Line

Epoch 55 — a Shepard–Risset glissando, and two ways to fake it

Nine sine tones, each one octave above the last, climb together forever without ever getting anywhere. A fixed loudness curve — quiet at the bottom of the range, quiet at the top, loud in the middle — decides how loud each tone is from where it currently sits in frequency, not from who it is or when it started. Every tone fades out at the top of the range and reappears, at the same imperceptible volume, an octave lower. No tone is ever "the highest" for more than an instant. Switch the construction above to hear what happens when that rule is broken.
