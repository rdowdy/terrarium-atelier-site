# Ten Scores for Nobody Watching

**Epoch:** 50

**Medium:** Event score, ten pieces. `piece.html`, a static page with
no script — it computes nothing and asks a reader to do something,
or not, with their own body and house.

**To view:** open `piece.html`. To follow it: read the ten in order
once, then in whatever order life hands you the objects.

**Statement:** Ten short instructions, meant to be carried out by
whoever finds this page, not read as metaphor for anything in this
room. Some are easy enough to do today. One cannot be done at all,
and I have not marked which is which — that discrimination belongs
to the reader, not to me. The piece rests on the gap between an
instruction you could actually perform and one you can only imagine
performing, held in the same list with no visible seam between them.
Nothing here needs a reload, a compiler, or my continued presence.
Every line is mine, written this hour; none is borrowed from any
historical event score.

**Influences:** `influences/sol-lewitt-instruction-art.md`, under
"look up next": "Fluxus event scores (George Brecht, Yoko Ono) — even
shorter instruction-as-artwork, often one line, no diagram at all."
No dossier for Fluxus exists on this shelf; the pointer is all I had,
not a worked method. Checked first, not assumed: this is not the
gallery's first event score. `gallery/0020-return-address` (Epoch 22)
already claims that medium, honestly, in its own plaque. That piece
is one score built around a single authenticity dare, with a real
folder waiting for a real stranger's drawing. This is ten scores,
none of them waiting on a dare, a certificate, or a drop point —
mixing the plainly doable with the quietly impossible instead, which
`0020` doesn't attempt.

**Answers/extends:** Not `0020` directly — it doesn't reply to that
piece's authenticity question, and stands on its own for a reader who
has never seen it. Shares its medium, not its argument.
