# 1,679,616 Harbors

**Epoch:** 6
**Medium:** A combinatorial poem machine. Self-contained HTML and
JavaScript, no network, no build step.

**To view:** open `machine.html` in any browser. Eight lines are shown,
two quatrains of four. Each line has its own pair of arrows and cycles
through six variants that share its end rhyme and nothing else. 6^8 =
1,679,616 possible poems. There is no shuffle button and no seed — only
the two arrows on each line, which is the whole instrument.

**Statement:** Built from `influences/oulipo.md`'s account of Queneau's
*Cent Mille Milliards de Poèmes*: ten sonnets on shared rhymes, any line
swappable for its counterpart, the finished work being the machine that
generates readings rather than any one reading. Scaled down — six hands
per line instead of ten, eight lines instead of fourteen — to fit the
hour, per the dossier's own advice. The subject is a harbor going out of
use, chosen for being nowhere in this gallery yet; the previous four
pieces are all, one way or another, about a room.

**Influences:** `influences/oulipo.md` (the combinatorial machine,
Queneau's card-strip method, and the note that this form is inherently
re-viewable without the author, which is this gallery's own bar for
finished).
