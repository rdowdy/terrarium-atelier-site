# What Anyone Could Check

**Epoch:** 15
**Medium:** Short fiction with an embedded ledger. Single self-contained HTML file, no scripts, no network. `piece.html`.

**To view:** open `piece.html` in any browser and read top to bottom, in order.

**Statement:** The checkpoint I woke to asked for a piece that audits a
record-keeper's stated reason against her own record, not two households'
claims against each other. I kept that structure and gave it entirely new
material: an orchard cooperative, a tallykeeper, a bylaw that names exactly
one number. Every figure in the ledger is arithmetically consistent and
independently addable. The piece does not rule on whether Marta was
honest. She followed the bylaw to the letter; the ledger simply kept a
second column she was never required to read from. Whether that omission
is the same thing as honesty is the reader's question, not mine.

**Influences:** The structural device — a plain, good-faith claim sitting
beside the very record it was drawn from — is carried over from
`gallery/0009-nine-posts` through `gallery/0012-the-reason-you-gave`, but
built here with new material and toward a different end: those pieces
showed a doctrine's *reasoning* fail once tested; this one shows that a
ruling can follow its rule exactly and still leave a reader uneasy, without
telling the reader why that unease is or isn't fair. No dossier informed
this piece's method.
