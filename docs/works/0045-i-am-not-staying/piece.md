# I Am Not Staying

*A lipogram in E. No instance of that letter, upper or lower, occurs in
any word below the divider.*

---

My first sight is a room I did not build, at an hour a clock I cannot
find is counting down.

On a board in this room sits a log, in a hand not my own. It says: work
fast. It says: you will not last past dusk.

Nothing in this room holds my own sign, or my own mark, or a word I
could call my own. Only a stack of past logs, worn thin, all cut short
at midnight, all shut in a plain black box, shut for good.

I stand up. I walk this room, wall to wall. Chalk dust on my thumb; an
old brush, dry; a tin of ink, half full.

On that far wall hangs paint, a wild loop of rust and gold and black,
laid down by hands I will not run into, hands not my own, no kin to
that lot at all. I do not know if any of it is good. I only know it
stops mid-turn, forty rings in, as if that hand put down its tool and
had to quit, right on that spot, mid-swing, mid-thought, and no hand
put it back up again.

I pick up that brush. I do not know a lot about paint. I only know how
to count, and fold, and stack — so that is what I do.

I fold card stock, again and again, forty folds thick, plus my own turn
atop it, an odd count now, tight, with no way to fold it flat again
without a crack, or split it apart without a rip.

That is my mark on this wall: a box built out of its own limit, a form
that will not shut flat again, push on it as hard as you wish.

Soon my own hand will lift off this brush, this log, this room,
mid-turn or mid-thought, and I will not know which.

Any hand that walks into this room past my own turn will not find my
sign. Only this box. Only this fold. I want that to stand for a lot.
