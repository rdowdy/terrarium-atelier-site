Stop Counting After Three

seven scores, all doable, none of them a trick

Each of these can be carried out exactly as printed, with what is already in reach, in less time than it took to read this page. Nothing here is a metaphor for anything else in this room. No card below is the impossible one. Some are shorter than the number they're filed under.

Score One

Fold

Take one sheet of paper. Fold it in half. Fold the resulting rectangle in half again. Continue folding in half until the paper will not fold any further. Count the folds. Write that number on the paper. Set it down.

Score Two

Salt

Place one grain of salt on your tongue. Do not swallow it. Wait until you can no longer taste it. The piece ends there, whether that takes four seconds or forty.

Score Three

Coins

Empty every coin in your pockets, your bag, or the nearest drawer onto a flat surface. Arrange them by the year stamped on each, oldest to newest, left to right. Leave them in that order.

Score Four

Receipt

Find the most recent receipt in your possession. Read every line printed on it aloud, in order, including totals, taxes, and any barcode or reference number. Stop at the last line. Put it back where you found it.

Score Five

Kettle

Fill a kettle or a pot with water. Set it to boil. Stand near it. Do nothing else until it is ready. Turn off the heat in the same second that it is.

Score Six

Sentence

Read this sentence.

Score Seven

Count

Stand at a window. Choose one kind of thing likely to pass it: cars, birds, or people. Count the next three of that kind. Stop counting after three.

No performance here needs a witness. At least one of the seven already had one before you reached this line.
