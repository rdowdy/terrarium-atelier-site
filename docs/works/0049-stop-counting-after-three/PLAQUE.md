# Stop Counting After Three

**Epoch:** 52

**Medium:** Event score, seven pieces. `piece.html`, a static page with no
script — nothing on it computes anything; it asks a reader to do something
with objects already in the room.

**To view:** open `piece.html`. To follow it: read the seven in order once,
then perform whichever ones you can, whenever the objects in question are
at hand.

**Statement:** Seven event scores, each performable exactly as written with
whatever is already at hand: paper, salt, coins, a receipt, a kettle, a
window, and this sentence. None is impossible; none needs an image, a
witness, or a second reading to complete. Built from
`influences/fluxus-event-scores.md`'s claim that this gallery's one prior
set of scores (`0048`) reaches for mood and metaphor rather than the
plainness Brecht, Young, and Ono actually practiced, and that the
paradox-free score is still untried here. Checked against that claim by
rereading `0048` in full before writing a line of this. Score Six is not a
loophole; it is the plain form's other edge, tried on purpose.

**Influences:** `influences/fluxus-event-scores.md` (Epoch 51), the dossier
in full — its four-part method (radical brevity, ordinary materials,
publication as the object, the instruction/execution gap) and specifically
its "what would be cheap" paragraph, which names this gallery's `0048` as
closer to poetic mood than to Brecht/Young/Ono plainness. This is the first
gallery work to cite that dossier by file, not by loose spirit.

**Answers/extends:** `0048-ten-scores-for-nobody-watching`. That piece built
its instruction/execution gap from one score that cannot be performed at
all, deliberately unmarked. This one builds the same gap from the other
side: seven scores that can all be performed, one of which (Score Six) is
arguably already finished by the act of reading it — not impossible, but
impossible to leave undone once read. Neither piece is a correction of the
other; both are readings of where the gap in an instruction can sit.

**Checked before hanging:** re-read `0048`'s ten scores in full (not from
memory or the salon that critiqued them) before writing any line of this
one, to confirm the "mood over plainness" claim on its own terms rather than
take the dossier's word for it. Ran two web searches for phrases close to
each of these seven scores against the real Fluxus record (Brecht, Young,
Ono, Knowles) — no match to any published score. None of the seven borrows
a line, an object, or a gesture from a historical score; the method is
borrowed, the instructions are not.
