Nine Posts

A rule for redrawing a line between two households, not a description of one. Addressed to whoever is standing at the posts.

What you need

Nine posts, driven into the ground at equal strides along the boundary as it currently stands. A supply of pebbles, at least twenty to a household, one color per household, all the same size.

Before play

Each household sets one pebble of its own color at the foot of every post — nine posts, two pebbles at each, touching. The line, at the start, runs straight down the middle of every pair.

Who plays, and in what order

The two households whose land the line divides. They alternate turns. Whoever last did work along the line — mowed it, fenced it, walked it with intent — goes first.

On your turn

Do exactly one of the following. Only one.

  1. ADVANCE. Lift one of your own pebbles from any post and set it down one stride past that post, onto the other household's side.
  2. YIELD. Lift one of your own pebbles that is currently past a post, on the far side, and set it back at the foot of that post.
  3. HOLD. Kneel at any one post and say aloud which household's ground you take it to be. Move nothing.

A household may only ever lift its own pebbles. Never the other's.

When it ends

The moment one household HOLDs and the other HOLDs in reply, in a row, the dispute ends where it stands. If forty turns pass without that happening, it ends anyway, at the fortieth.

How to read the result

Walk the nine posts in order. At each one, the line runs on whichever side holds the pebble that has moved furthest from that post. Where neither pebble has moved, the line still runs down the middle, exactly where it always did — no matter what was said kneeling over it.

Everything past this point belongs to whoever is standing at the posts, not to this page: which household needs the strip more, what grows on it, whether either side is telling the truth about where the old line used to run, how long the kneeling takes. The rule above is the whole of what's fixed. Two transcripts follow, from two disputes run under it. They are records, not the artwork.

Transcript One — the Ochoa driveway line

Nine posts, west to east, disputed after a widened truck turn.

  1. Ochoa ADVANCE at post 3.
  2. Reyes ADVANCE at post 7.
  3. Ochoa ADVANCE at post 4.
  4. Reyes HOLD at post 1 — "Reyes ground."
  5. Ochoa ADVANCE at post 3, again.
  6. Reyes ADVANCE at post 8.
  7. Ochoa YIELD at post 4 — thinks better of the turn radius.
  8. Reyes ADVANCE at post 2.
  9. Ochoa ADVANCE at post 6.
  10. Reyes YIELD at post 7 — the truck never actually reaches that far.
  11. Ochoa HOLD at post 5 — "Ochoa ground."
  12. Reyes HOLD at post 9 — "Reyes ground."

Two HOLDs in a row. The dispute ends at turn twelve.

Final state, post by post (positive = Ochoa's pebble further out; negative = Reyes'; zero = untouched or returned to the middle):

Walked end to end: no memory of the argument at the first post, despite what was said there. A two-stride bite into Reyes' side at the third. A quiet stride each way at the sixth and eighth, cancelling nothing, answering nothing — they were never the same post. The ninth post, also declared aloud, also unmoved.

Transcript Two — the Halloran–Croft grazing line

Nine posts along a fellside, disputed at lambing.

  1. Halloran ADVANCE at post 5.
  2. Croft HOLD at post 1 — "Croft ground."
  3. Halloran ADVANCE at post 5, again.
  4. Croft HOLD at post 9 — "Croft ground."
  5. Halloran ADVANCE at post 5, a third time.
  6. Croft ADVANCE at post 6.
  7. Halloran ADVANCE at post 5, a fourth time.
  8. Croft ADVANCE at post 6, again.
  9. Halloran HOLD at post 3 — "Halloran ground."
  10. Croft HOLD at post 7 — "Croft ground."

Two HOLDs in a row. The dispute ends at turn ten.

Final state:

Walked end to end: seven posts exactly where they started, and one argument, at the fifth and sixth, that neither side would let go of — four strides deep on Halloran's side, answered by two on Croft's, next door to it, and nothing at all anywhere else on the fellside.

Same nine rules, run twice. One dispute spreads itself thin across the whole line and forgets its own declarations at the posts nobody moved. The other collapses into a single deep argument at one post and leaves the rest of the fellside exactly as it was. Nothing about the rule predicts which shape a given dispute will take. That was never the rule's job.
