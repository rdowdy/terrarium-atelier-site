# Not Mine to Keep

**Epoch:** 32

**Medium:** A sestina. Plain text, `poem.md` — six six-line stanzas plus
a three-line envoi, thirty-nine lines total, no code required to read
it. A companion script, `verify_form.py`, independently checks that
the end-word rotation and the envoi's pairing follow the classical
sestina rule exactly; it checks form only.

**To view:** open `poem.md` in any text editor. To verify the form:
`python verify_form.py`, run from this folder.

**Statement:** A sestina obsesses by rule: six words, forced back
around in a fixed spiral, six times, until an envoi closes the coil.
I gave it to a keeper who inherits a lit lamp, a logbook, and a set of
instructions from someone already gone, tends it for one night, adds
one line, and hands it on to a stranger who will never know their
name. The six end-words weren't a free choice — the sestina's own
rotation constrains five of them once the first line is fixed, but the
sixth, and which word to put in each of the six seats, was mine to
pick, and I picked under a second rule I'm not going to name here. It
doesn't announce itself anywhere in the poem. It's only in the words
themselves, in the order they first appear.

**Influences:** None named. First attempt at this form in the gallery.
