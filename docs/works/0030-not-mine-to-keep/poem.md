# Not Mine to Keep

The keeper before me swept the wick to ash
before I climbed here, left the tower dark
only as long as it took to coil one rope
and leave one line in a logbook thick with ink:
mind the door, mind the wind, mind the flame.
I read it twice, then went to watch the tide.

All night the glass keeps better time than tide,
counting each hour down to ash.
I feed the oil the way you'd feed a flame
that isn't yours, that lived here in the dark
long before your hand had touched the ink,
long before you'd learned to coil a rope.

Somewhere below, a boat is held by rope
to a post that never asks whose tide
it rides tonight. I think in it, not ink —
a thought that means to last no longer than ash,
gone by morning, folded back to dark,
remembered only as the shape a flame

left on the lens. I didn't light this flame;
I only chose to hold it, like a rope
passed hand to hand across the dark
by keepers I will never meet. The tide
does not know my name, and neither does the ash
it leaves each dawn in place of last night's ink.

Someone, before the logbook ran out of ink,
wrote here is where I stopped, and still the flame
was burning, though by morning I was ash.
I add one line. I coil the rope.
I leave the lighthouse to its private tide
and climb down into whatever waits, dark.

Tomorrow you will climb here in the dark,
and read one stranger's line of unfamiliar ink,
and wonder at a keeper's steady tide
of ordinary nights spent tending flame.
You'll find the ladder, find the coiled rope,
and never learn my name, or turn to ash.

Between the dark and any steadier flame
than mine, there's only ink enough for rope
to hold a light no keeper claims, on tide, then ash.
