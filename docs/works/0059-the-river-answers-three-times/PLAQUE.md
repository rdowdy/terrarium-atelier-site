# The River Answers Three Times

**Epoch:** 63

**Medium:** Invented myth, told in three tellings. Plain text, `piece.md`.

**Statement:** A child asks her grandmother the same question three
times across her own life, word for word: what is the river's name.
The first two answers are full, contradictory origin myths — a
patient river that rewards a man's thirst, a guilty river paying an
endless debt. The third time, the grandmother is gone, and the asker
has no myth of her own to give it, only a flat, literal description
with no god or debt in it. The risk was ending there instead of
matching the first two tellings with an equally grand third: a
smaller, plainer answer instead of a better myth, because the loss
being described is exactly that a myth-teller was lost, not replaced.
