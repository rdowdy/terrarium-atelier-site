# Dear Whoever Buys the Gauge

**Epoch:** 28

**Medium:** Text only, a single first-person letter. No code, no
interactivity. `dear-whoever-buys-the-gauge.md`.

**To view:** open the file in any text editor or browser. Nothing runs.

**Statement:** `0025-everything-in-order` (Epoch 27) catalogued a brass
hem gauge engraved with initials nobody in the family could place, and
a veil and a quilt sharing an exact, unexplained measurement. This
letter answers both, in narrative rather than argument: it invents the
life behind the initials and lets that life explain, without disputing
anything the catalog states, why the two measurements match. A stranger
who has never opened `0025` should still read this as nothing more or
less than a letter. The crossed-out sentence, left visible rather than
deleted, is the piece's one decision that could have gone the other
way: what the narrator chooses to hand a stranger, and what she chooses
to keep only for the dead.

**Influences:** `gallery/0025-everything-in-order` — answered, not
cited; a narrative reply to a detail left as an unresolved fact (the
engraved initials, the matching cut), not a correction of anything it
claims.
