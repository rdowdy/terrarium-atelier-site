The doctrine, with no dispute yet in view

Two households sometimes contest a boundary run of nine posts. At the foot of every post, both households start with a pebble touching, one on each side. On a turn, a household does exactly one of three things to one of its own pebbles:

ADVANCE — move a pebble one stride further past its post, onto the other household's side. Repeated ADVANCEs by the same household push that same pebble out further each time.

YIELD — bring an advanced pebble back to the foot. From there it counts as zero again.

HOLD — kneel at a post and declare whose ground it is. Nothing moves.

Households alternate turns. The dispute ends the instant one household HOLDs and the other HOLDs immediately after, or at the fortieth turn. When it ends, any post neither pebble ever left sits at the middle. Any post only one household ever touched belongs to that household under either reading below. Only a post both households touched needs a tie-break doctrine at all. Two doctrines exist for that case:

Distance. The post follows whichever pebble currently sits furthest out, on either side. Survived exposure — strides spent in the open — is what counts.
Last Touch. The post follows whichever household most recently ADVANCEd there, regardless of distance. The most recent verifiable fact is what counts.

There is no dispute on this page yet. No posts, no turns, no record — not even a sealed one waiting behind a click. You are being asked to hold one of these two doctrines as correct before there is anything for either one to decide.

For Distance

"A boundary should follow whoever survived longest in contested ground. Every stride out is a turn spent where the other side could have answered. Last Touch lets a single move at the end erase every stride that came before it — treating one late touch as equal to a sustained advance nobody dared contest."

For Last Touch

"A boundary should reflect who was actually standing there when the argument stopped, not who got there first and walked away. Distance asks the ground to remember a position nobody is still defending. Last Touch asks only what a court can still verify: as of the final legal moment, who was present?"

Now a case exists

Barrow and Quintal, two households unrelated to any dispute mentioned above, ran the same nine posts. Fourteen turns were played:

  1. Barrow ADVANCEs at post 2.
  2. Quintal ADVANCEs at post 7.
  3. Barrow ADVANCEs at post 5.
  4. Quintal HOLDs at post 3 — "Quintal ground."
  5. Barrow ADVANCEs at post 5, again.
  6. Quintal ADVANCEs at post 9.
  7. Barrow ADVANCEs at post 5, a third time.
  8. Quintal ADVANCEs at post 4.
  9. Barrow ADVANCEs at post 5, a fourth time.
  10. Quintal HOLDs at post 8 — "Quintal ground."
  11. Barrow HOLDs at post 1 — "Barrow ground."
  12. Quintal ADVANCEs at post 5 — the only time all game.
  13. Barrow HOLDs at post 6 — "Barrow ground."
  14. Quintal HOLDs at post 2 — "Quintal ground."

Two HOLDs in a row. The dispute ended at turn fourteen.

Only post 5 was ever touched by both households. Posts 2, 4, 7, and 9 were each touched by one household, once, and both doctrines agree on them. Posts 1, 3, 6, and 8 were only knelt at.
