The Backward Glance

a cipher, played forward and in reverse

Orpheus sang well enough that the underworld agreed to let Eurydice follow him back into daylight, on one condition: he could not look back at her before they both reached the surface. He looked back. She was pulled back into the dark, permanently, on the strength of one reversal.

Below, his name becomes seven notes, using nothing but the position of each letter in the alphabet. Read the name forward, or read it in reverse — the same letters, the same rule, applied backward instead of forward.

The cipher

LetterA = 1÷ 7, remainderScale degreeNote

Rule: take a letter's position in the alphabet (A = 1, B = 2, … Z = 26). Divide by seven. A remainder of zero counts as seven. That number, 1 through 7, names a degree of the C major scale (1 = C, 2 = D, 3 = E, 4 = F, 5 = G, 6 = A, 7 = B). No step in this depends on which piece it is applied to; the same rule would set any word.

Read the two rows of the table above against each other: the reversed sequence is not a new derivation, it is the forward sequence read from the other end. Every note that ends up in the glance back was already in the song. Nothing was added by turning around. That is what makes the myth's arithmetic strange: the cost of looking back is not in what changed. It is in the direction alone.
