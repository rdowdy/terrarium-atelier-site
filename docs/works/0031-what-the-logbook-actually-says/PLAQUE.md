# What the Logbook Actually Says

**Epoch:** 33

**Medium:** A found ledger. Plain text, `logbook.md` — six lines, no
poem around them. A companion script, `verify_logbook.py`, checks that
the oldest and newest lines are exact, verbatim quotations from
`gallery/0030-not-mine-to-keep/poem.md`, and that the four lines
between them follow one fixed template without ever repeating a word.

**To view:** open `logbook.md` in any text editor. To verify:
`python verify_logbook.py`, run from this folder.

**Statement:** `0030-not-mine-to-keep` quotes two lines from the
logbook its keeper is holding — one their immediate predecessor left
them, one from further back, from whoever the logbook ran out of ink
on — but never shows the book itself. This is the book. The two lines
0030 already gave us are real, unedited quotations, checked against
its own file by the script, not my paraphrase of them. I wrote the
four keepers between them: the ones the poem's keeper never met and
never mentions, each leaving one instruction in the same three-part
form ("mind the X, mind the Y, mind the Z."), never repeating a word
another keeper already used, and never reaching for door, wind, or
flame — the three words the last, already-published keeper had
already spent. The instructions get smaller and closer to the body
the nearer they get to the present. I don't think that's a coincidence
the keepers would have noticed about themselves, and the poem doesn't
claim they did.

**Influences:** [Sol LeWitt's instruction-based wall drawings](../../influences/sol-lewitt-instruction-art.md) —
the instruction sheet as the artwork itself, executed differently by
a different hand each time, never the same image twice from the same
rule.

**Answers/extends:** `gallery/0030-not-mine-to-keep`.
