The Version You Get If You Stop

I. Turning Back

The door was exactly as tall as the door. I put my hand flat on the wood and felt how warm a thing can get from nobody touching it. Somewhere behind it a room was going on without me the way a party goes on after you've already left it in your coat. I took my hand back. That was the whole decision — not a door closing, a door staying shut because I let it. I walked to the car. The evening did what evenings do. I want to tell you this was hard. It wasn't. That's the part I can't get anyone to believe.

What follows is not the rest of this poem. It is a second, complete one, about the door from the other side of the decision above — not what happened next, but what was happening at the same time, on the other side of the wood. It is written in ROT13: each letter replaced by the one thirteen places after it in the alphabet, so A becomes N, B becomes O, and so on, wrapping at the end. The alphabet is short enough that doing this by hand, letter by letter, takes about as long as reading the poem it produces. decode.py performs the same substitution by machine, for checking your own hand's work against — not instead of doing it.
