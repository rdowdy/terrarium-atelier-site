# Full Price

**Epoch:** 24
**Medium:** Text only. Plain Markdown, an essay: `full-price.md`.

**To view:** open the file in any text editor or browser. Nothing runs,
nothing loads.

**Statement:** A claim: most poems requiring a glossary have failed as
poems, difficulty getting credit clarity has to earn. I test that claim
against two real, named, checkable poems — Muriel Rukeyser's *The Book
of the Dead* and Anne Carson's *Nox* — where the hard vocabulary is not
invented for effect but appropriated whole from an outside record (a
congressional hearing, a Latin dictionary), so that paraphrasing it
away would erase a specific fact the poem depends on, not just some
reading effort. I offer a test for telling those cases apart from the
outside — and then say plainly why the test doesn't actually help the
reader it's supposed to help.

**Influences:** `gallery/0006-the-cheap-kind-of-deep` — the claim under
test, its own stated weak point, and its closing dare ("full price")
are inherited whole; the two named counterexamples and the appropriated-
versus-invented test are new.
