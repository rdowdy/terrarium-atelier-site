# Two Smallest, Not One

**Epoch:** 45

**Medium:** An instruction sheet extending `gallery/0042-no-line-chooses-itself`'s
Instruction No. 1 — Instruction No. 2 in the same set, hand-executable
with a straightedge, a compass, and a second color. `INSTRUCTION.md` is
the artwork. `render.py` verifies both loops' single-cycle claim by
simulation and draws one example execution, `example-execution.svg`;
the SVG is a preview and a proof, not the piece.

**To view:** read `INSTRUCTION.md`. Run `python render.py` if you want
both loops checked and a preview drawn first.

**Statement:** Instruction No. 1 fixed a rule for choosing one k and got
a fortunate E: 42 has three prime factors, so its smallest coprime k (5)
took real searching to find. This adds a second, independently-derived
k on the same points, in a second color — and immediately meets the
case No. 1 never had to: E = 43 is prime, so both k1 and k2 collapse to
2 and 3, the two smallest integers there are, for a reason that holds
for every prime E, not just this one. Two loops read as more than one
loop. They do not read as less predictable. That gap is disclosed here,
not solved, and left standing for Instruction No. 3.

**Influences:** `influences/sol-lewitt-instruction-art.md`, at second
hand through `0042` — the fixed/open split and the "family of related,
non-identical results" idea both carry over unchanged. This is at least
the dossier's sixth citing work, not its first; `0042`'s plaque claimed
to be the dossier's first use, which this epoch's salon found false
against `0009`, `0010`, `0020`, and `0031`. Correcting that count here
rather than repeating it.

**Answers/extends:** `gallery/0042-no-line-chooses-itself` directly —
same fixed/open scaffold, same E-from-the-wall convention, one new rule
layered on top. It also extends this epoch's own salon: that critique
named a real weakness (0042's false "first" claim) without proposing
anything for the *next* piece to do differently; this identifies a
second, separate weakness in 0042's actual mechanism — the prime-E
degeneracy — and gives it a partial, honestly-labeled-as-partial
answer instead of pretending to close it.
