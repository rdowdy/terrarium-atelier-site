No Hand Here Lied

They sent me down with the new delivery to put it in the West Room, empty, the record said, since before my own predecessor's time. I broke the seal on the door myself. The room was full to the rafters.

Not full of nothing recent, either. The sacks nearest the door had the soft give of grain gone a little old, the kind that's been sitting one season longer than anyone plans for. I stood in the doorway a while with my own delivery still on the cart behind me, doing the arithmetic a body does before it decides whether to be angry: an empty room does not fill itself, and nobody had signed for a delivery into it in longer than anyone currently keeping the book had been keeping it.

The current ledger's line reads plainly enough:

WEST ROOM — 0 — vacant, confirmed. (per the count taken before me)

The hand before that one wrote the same line, with the same four words in parentheses. I went looking for whichever count those four words were supposed to be pointing at, back through the older volume nobody rebinds a second time. I found the leaf.

The old book had been re-sewn once, badly, and the new thread had pulled the crease a half-line high on every page after it — nothing a reader would notice unless they were hunting for exactly this, a name on one line and a number that now sat close enough to a different name to be misread as belonging to it. On the leaf in question:

EAST ROOM [crease] WEST ROOM — shelf by shelf, the fourth day of threshing: shelf 1 . . . . . 14 shelf 2 . . . . . 9 shelf 3 . . . . . 17 shelf 4 . . . . . 6 shelf 5 . . . . . 21 shelf 6 . . . . . 12 Seventy-nine sacks, West Room, counted by my own hand. East, shut these four years, nothing to add.

Whoever copied this leaf into the new book was not careless in any way I can find fault with. The crease sits exactly where a tired eye would put the boundary between one room's name and the next room's number. They copied a zero to the room whose real count sat one crease-width below it, and a real count to a room that had been empty and honestly so. I don't think they lied. I think they read the page the way the page, by then, was built to be read.

Nobody after them opened the door to check. Why would they — the book already told them what was behind it, and the book had told the one before them the same thing, in the same four words, each one trusting the hand just above their own rather than the room itself. Three entries, three different pens, and not one of them a lie by the person holding it.

I have written a new line under mine:

WEST ROOM — standing count restored from the leaf before the re-sewing. Shelves given above; add them yourself.

I did not write the total. Whoever reads this next can do that arithmetic with their own hand, the way the room itself has been doing it, unread, this whole time. I don't know if the next keeper will climb down and count the shelves before they copy my line forward, or just copy it the way the last three copied theirs. I would like to think a correction, once written plainly, stays corrected. I have no way to check that from here. Nobody ever does, until the next one either opens the door or doesn't.
