# Undated

*A one-act play. Two characters: MARA, forties. THEO, late thirties. A
workshop attached to a garage — a workbench, pegboard with tool outlines
where the tools no longer hang, three shelves of mason jars full of
screws, nails, unlabeled hardware. Late afternoon light through one window.
Three cardboard boxes on the floor, marked in marker: KEEP, SELL, DONATE.
The DONATE box is nearly full.*

**THEO:** *(holding up a hand plane, turning it over)* This one's good.
Stanley. He kept it oiled.

**MARA:** Keep or sell.

**THEO:** Keep. Somebody should keep a plane like this.

**MARA:** You don't have a workbench.

**THEO:** I could get a workbench.

*MARA doesn't answer. She's working through the shelf of jars, tipping
each one into her palm, checking, pouring it back. Behind the third jar
from the left, wedged flat against the shelf board, there's a tobacco tin,
rust at the seam. She works it loose without looking like she's looking
for anything.*

**THEO:** Remember when he tried to teach you dovetails and you cried.

**MARA:** I didn't cry.

**THEO:** You cried. You were nine. You threw the chisel.

**MARA:** I threw it because he moved my hand without asking.

*She opens the tin one-handed, in her lap, angled away from Theo, who is
now testing the plane against a scrap of pine, not watching her. Inside:
a photograph, small, the kind with a white border, and nothing else.
She looks at it for four seconds. It is their father, younger than either
of them has ever known him, an arm around a woman who is not their
mother, in front of a car neither of them recognizes. Nothing is written
on the back.*

**THEO:** This one's still sharp. Feel that.

**MARA:** *(not looking up yet)* Mm.

**THEO:** Feel it.

*She closes the tin. Slides it into the KEEP box, under a folded canvas
apron, not into her pocket, not announced. She stands, crosses to him,
runs her thumb along the blade he's holding out.*

**MARA:** Sharp.

**THEO:** Keep, then.

**MARA:** Keep.

*He sets the plane in the KEEP box, on top, where it's visible. Neither
of them touches the tin again for the rest of the scene.*

**THEO:** What do we do with the jars.

**MARA:** Sell the lot. Nobody wants a jar of somebody else's screws.

**THEO:** Feels wrong to sell his screws.

**MARA:** They're not his screws anymore, Theo. They're screws.

*Beat.*

**THEO:** You okay?

**MARA:** I'm sorting a garage.

**THEO:** That's not what I asked.

**MARA:** *(lifting the last jar, checking it, setting it in SELL)* I know
what you asked.

*He watches her a second longer than the moment needs, then goes back to
the pegboard, lifting tools down one at a time, calling out each one for
her to sort. She answers KEEP or SELL or DONATE without looking up again,
steady, unremarkable, correct every time. The light through the window has
moved half a pane by the time the pegboard is bare. They carry the three
boxes out together, the KEEP box between them, each holding one end. The
workshop door swings shut on its own weight. Nobody locks it.*

**END.**
